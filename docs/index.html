Predicting Near-Term Organic Search Decline

Author: Long Nguyen · Lane: Growth / Recovery / Momentum Prediction — Content Decline Detection · Date: 2026-08-14

Abstract. Can historical search-performance and content-lifecycle signals identify content pages likely to experience a meaningful decline in organic search impressions over the following 30 days? This project uses the FlyRank pseudonymized warehouse and a page-client prediction grain, with March 2026 features and April 2026 outcomes. Logistic Regression uses five pre-decision features and initially improves the transparent review baseline at the top of the ranking. However, grouped-by-client validation produces a lower and more conservative estimate of generalization. The final output is therefore a human-reviewed decision-support queue rather than an autonomous content-management system.

1. Problem framing

The decision is which content pages an SEO/content team should inspect first because their historical signals indicate elevated measured risk of near-term organic-impression decline.

The unit of analysis is one content page for one client at one prediction point. The output is a ranked risk score, reason code, and suggested review action.

Can historical search-performance and content-lifecycle signals identify pages likely to experience a >20% decline in organic search impressions over the next 30 days?

The feature window is 2026-03-01 to 2026-03-31; the prediction point is 2026-04-01; and the target window is 2026-04-01 to 2026-04-30.

2. Data safety

The analysis uses the FlyRank internship warehouse: dim_clients, dim_content, fact_content_daily_performance, and fact_content_query_90d. The daily fact table contains 78,835,655 rows covering 2025-01-27 through 2026-06-30.

The final five model features were imp_prev30, clk_prev30, avg_position_prev30, days_with_impressions, and content_age_days.

Future measurements, label-derived fields, product decision fields, and pseudonymous identifiers as predictive inputs were excluded. A deliberate leakage test using impression_change_pct raised ROC-AUC from 0.65 to 1.00, confirming that the audit could detect future-window leakage; the feature was removed.

6. Interpretation

Grouped permutation importance placed days_with_impressions first (0.0980), followed by clk_prev30 (0.0695). The remaining features had much smaller measured contributions.

The Week-5 error analysis contained 4,874 false positives and 2,280 false negatives. High-scoring false positives show why model scores require human review: high historical exposure does not guarantee future decline.

The freshness audit was not monotonic: measured decline risk was 46.63% for pages ≤90 days old, 55.05% for 91–180 days, 53.83% for 181–365 days, and 52.54% for >365 days. Therefore the evidence does not support a simple “older always means riskier” claim.

7. Ranked recommendations

  1. High-risk + high-exposure: prioritize manual performance review.
  2. High-risk + stale + visible: review freshness and content relevance.
  3. High-risk + weak position: review search intent, relevance, and ranking context.
  4. High-risk + General manual review broader manual review.

Before acting, a reviewer should check search intent, relevance, freshness, search context, historical evidence, business context, technical issues, and measurement quality.

Do not automate: publishing, rewriting, deleting, changing search intent, changing canonical URLs, changing internal linking, changing metadata without review, declaring a ranking failure, claiming that a refresh will improve traffic, claiming Google's causal ranking mechanism, or overriding business priorities.
